# Lecture Notes: Chapter 19 – Vertical Integration and Outsourcing

## 1. Overview
This lecture explains how firms decide whether to produce internally or buy from external suppliers. The lecture focuses on vertical integration, outsourcing, transaction costs, asset specificity, hold-up problems, and contracting.

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Vertical Integration
A firm is vertically integrated when it participates in multiple stages of production.

#### Examples
- Producing inputs internally
- Owning distribution channels

#### Types
- **Backward integration:** Producing own inputs
- **Forward integration:** Moving closer to customers/distribution

---

### Outsourcing
Outsourcing means purchasing goods or services externally instead of producing internally.

#### Continuum of choices
1. Spot markets
2. Long-term contracts
3. Vertical integration

---

### Benefits of Competitive Markets

#### Economies of Scale
External suppliers may produce more efficiently due to specialization and larger production volumes.

#### Stronger Incentives
Competition motivates suppliers to reduce costs and improve efficiency.

#### Rule of thumb
If a competitive market exists, buying externally is often cheaper.

---

### Transaction Costs
Firms may avoid markets because using markets is costly.

#### Examples
- Searching for suppliers
- Negotiating contracts
- Enforcing contracts

If transaction costs become too high, vertical integration may be preferred.

---

### Firm-Specific Assets

#### Definition
Assets that are much more valuable inside a specific relationship than elsewhere.

#### Types
- **Site specificity**
- **Physical asset specificity**
- **Human asset specificity**
- **Dedicated asset specificity**

Higher asset specificity increases the likelihood of vertical integration.

---

### Hold-Up Problem
Occurs when one party becomes dependent after making a specific investment.

#### Example
A supplier invests in specialized equipment and the buyer later renegotiates prices aggressively.

#### Consequences
- Lower investment incentives
- Need for more detailed contracts
- Increased transaction costs

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Asset Specificity and Procurement Decision

#### Main idea
The choice between:
- Market transactions
- Contracts
- Vertical integration

depends on:
- Asset specificity
- Environmental uncertainty

#### General pattern
- Low specificity → market transactions
- Medium specificity → contracts
- High specificity → vertical integration

---

### Double Markup Problem

#### What it is
Two independent firms each add a markup.

#### Result
- Higher final prices
- Lower output
- Lower combined profits

#### Solution
Vertical integration or contractual coordination.

---

### Price Discrimination via Vertical Integration

#### Idea
A producer may vertically integrate to prevent arbitrage between markets.

#### Example
Drug producer selling to:
- Cancer treatment market
- Pain reliever market

Integration prevents reselling between markets.

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Vertical Integration:** Producing across multiple production stages.
- **Outsourcing:** Buying externally.
- **Backward Integration:** Producing own inputs.
- **Forward Integration:** Controlling distribution.
- **Transaction Costs:** Costs of using markets.
- **Asset Specificity:** Assets with higher value inside a relationship.
- **Hold-Up Problem:** Opportunistic renegotiation after investments.
- **Double Markup:** Multiple firms adding margins.
- **Spot Market:** Short-term market transactions.
- **Long-Term Contract:** Repeated contractual relationship.
