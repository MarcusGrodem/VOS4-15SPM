# Lecture Notes: Chapter 10 – Incentive Conflicts and Contracts

## 1. Overview
This lecture explains how firms use contracts and organizational systems to reduce incentive conflicts. The chapter focuses on:
- Firms as a nexus of contracts
- Principal-agent relationships
- Incentive conflicts
- Moral hazard and adverse selection
- Agency costs
- Monitoring and bonding
- Implicit and reputational contracts

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Firms and Coordination
Markets allocate resources through:
- **Prices**

Firms allocate resources through:
- **Managers**

Because firms contain many decision makers:
- Coordination becomes necessary
- Incentives may become misaligned

Key issue:
- Employees maximize their own utility
- Firms want profit maximization

This creates incentive conflicts.

---

### The Firm as a Nexus of Contracts
The firm is viewed as:
- A collection of contracts between stakeholders

Stakeholders include:
- Employees
- Suppliers
- Customers
- Stockholders
- Banks
- Bondholders
- Labor unions

#### Key idea
The firm itself is not just a physical entity:
- It is a network of contractual relationships.

---

### Major Incentive Conflicts

#### 1. Shirking
Agents may:
- Avoid effort
- Reduce productivity

#### 2. Salaries and Perks
Managers may seek:
- High compensation
- Personal benefits/perks

#### 3. Risk Aversion
Managers may avoid risky investments even if profitable for owners.

#### 4. Different Time Horizons
Managers may prioritize:
- Short-term results
Instead of:
- Long-term firm value

#### 5. Overinvestment
Managers may engage in:
- Empire building
- Expanding firms unnecessarily

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Organizational Architecture Components
Contracts influence:
- **Decision rights**
- **Performance evaluation**
- **Reward systems**

These elements help align incentives.

---

### Agency Theory
#### Definition
An agency relationship occurs when:
- A principal hires an agent to act on their behalf.

#### Problem
Agents may pursue personal interests instead of the principal’s goals.

#### Examples
- Shareholders and CEOs
- Managers and employees

---

### Moral Hazard
#### Definition
Post-contractual behavior where agents act against the principal’s interest.

#### Example
- Shirking after being hired

#### Key feature
Caused by:
- Asymmetric information

The principal cannot fully observe effort.

---

### Adverse Selection
#### Definition
Pre-contractual information problem.

One party possesses private information before agreement.

#### Example
- A job applicant hiding low ability

#### Difference from moral hazard
- Adverse selection = before contract
- Moral hazard = after contract

---

### Costly Contracts
Contracts are costly because firms must:
- Negotiate
- Write
- Monitor
- Enforce agreements

#### Monitoring Costs
Costs incurred by principals to observe agents.

#### Bonding Costs
Costs incurred by agents to guarantee good behavior.

---

### Agency Cost Example: Good Tire and Brown & Brown
#### Without incentive problems
Efficient outcome:
- 50 hours of legal services

Both parties benefit.

#### With incentive problems
Potential problems:
- Lower effort
- Monitoring costs
- Bonding costs
- Residual loss

#### Residual loss
Loss that remains even after monitoring and bonding.

#### Key insight
Agency problems reduce total surplus.

---

### Implicit Contracts
#### Definition
Agreements that cannot legally be enforced.

#### Why they work
Because of:
- Reputation
- Trust
- Long-term relationships

#### Example
A firm promises future promotion opportunities.

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Principal:** Party hiring another party
- **Agent:** Party acting on behalf of another
- **Agency theory:** Study of conflicts between principals and agents
- **Moral hazard:** Post-contract opportunistic behavior
- **Adverse selection:** Pre-contract hidden information
- **Monitoring costs:** Costs of supervising agents
- **Bonding costs:** Costs agents incur to signal trustworthiness
- **Residual loss:** Remaining inefficiency from agency conflicts
- **Implicit contract:** Informal agreement based on reputation
