# Lecture Notes: Chapter 21 – The Economics of Regulation

## 1. Overview
This lecture explains why governments regulate markets and how regulation affects firms, industries, and society. It focuses on market failures, political incentives, and the economic theory of regulation.

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Government Regulation
Examples:
- Antitrust laws
- Environmental laws
- Financial disclosure requirements
- Labor and safety regulation

Regulation influences:
- Competition
- Incentives
- Organizational architecture

---

### Economic Motives for Regulation

Governments intervene to:
1. Protect property rights
2. Correct market failures

#### Types of market failures
- Externalities
- Public goods
- Monopoly power
- Asymmetric information

---

### Property Rights

#### Why important?
Clear property rights:
- Increase investment incentives
- Reduce transaction costs
- Support trade and contracts

Examples:
- Patents
- Copyrights
- Trademarks

---

### Externalities

#### Definition
Costs or benefits imposed on third parties.

#### Coase Theorem
If:
- Property rights are clear
- Transaction costs are low

then markets can solve externality problems efficiently.

#### Example
Pollution permits and carbon quotas.

---

### Public Goods

#### Characteristics
- Nonrival consumption
- Nonexcludable consumption

#### Problems
Free riders make it difficult to charge users directly.

Examples:
- National defense
- Lighthouses
- Public broadcasting

---

### Monopoly Regulation

#### Problem
Monopolies reduce output and increase prices.

#### Government responses
- Antitrust laws
- Price regulation

#### Risk
Regulators may become captured by industries.

---

### Asymmetric Information

#### Example
Used-car markets (“lemons”).

#### Consequences
- Adverse selection
- Market inefficiency

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Market for Regulation

#### Main idea
Regulation is influenced by:
- Firms
- Consumers
- Politicians

Politicians maximize political support.

#### Industry preference
Firms prefer monopoly profits.

#### Consumer preference
Consumers prefer lower prices.

---

### Political Support Functions

#### What it shows
Trade-off between:
- Firm profits
- Consumer prices
- Political support

#### Important result
Regulated price usually falls between:
- Competitive price
- Monopoly price

---

### Wealth Transfers via Regulation

#### Example
Taxi quotas.

#### Effects
- Higher prices
- Lower output
- Reduced consumer surplus
- Increased profits for producers

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Regulation:** Government intervention in markets.
- **Externality:** Third-party cost or benefit.
- **Coase Theorem:** Efficient bargaining with low transaction costs.
- **Public Good:** Nonrival and nonexcludable good.
- **Monopoly:** Single dominant seller.
- **Asymmetric Information:** Unequal information between parties.
- **Adverse Selection:** Bad products drive out good products.
- **Regulatory Capture:** Regulators influenced by industry.
- **Political Support Function:** Relationship between regulation and political support.
