## 2. Main Notes

### What is Game Theory?
- **Game theory** studies strategic interaction between rational decision makers.
- Managers must:
  - Think about how competitors will react
  - Anticipate rival decisions
  - Make decisions under strategic uncertainty

#### Assumptions
- Decision makers are **rational**
- Each player attempts to maximize their own payoff
- Firms anticipate reactions from rivals
- Focus in this chapter: **non-cooperative games**

---

### Simultaneous-Move, Non-Repeated Games
#### Simultaneous Move
- Firms make decisions **without knowing** the rival’s decision.

#### Non-Repeated Interaction
- The game happens only once.

#### Example: Boeing vs Airbus Pricing
Both firms choose:
- **High price**
- **Low price**

Key idea:
- If one firm chooses a low price while the other chooses a high price, the low-price firm wins the contract.
- If both choose the same pricing strategy, profits are shared.

---

### Dominant Strategy
- A **dominant strategy** is the best choice regardless of what the rival does.

#### Example
Airbus has a dominant strategy:
- Choose **Low Price**

Boeing:
- Does **not** have a dominant strategy.

#### Exam relevance
You may be asked to:
- Identify dominant strategies from a payoff matrix
- Explain why a strategy is dominant

---

### Nash Equilibrium
- A **Nash equilibrium** exists when:
  - Each player chooses the best strategy given the rival’s strategy
  - No player has an incentive to change alone

#### Important characteristics
- Stable outcome
- Firms are responding optimally to each other

#### Example
If Airbus chooses low price and Boeing also chooses low price:
- Neither firm gains by changing strategy alone.

#### How to identify
- Use the **circle technique**
- Identify each player’s best response

#### Exam use
In an exam:
1. Explain each player’s incentives
2. Identify best responses
3. Locate equilibrium
4. Explain why it is stable

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Dominant Strategy Model
#### What it is
A strategy that gives the highest payoff regardless of rival behavior.

#### What it is used for
Predicting firm behavior in strategic situations.

#### Exam application
- Compare payoffs row-by-row and column-by-column.
- Show why one strategy dominates alternatives.

---

### Nash Equilibrium
#### What it is
A set of strategies where all players are making the best possible decision given rivals’ actions.

#### Used for
Predicting stable strategic outcomes.

#### Exam application
- Analyze strategic interaction
- Explain stability of equilibrium
- Discuss whether firms have incentives to deviate

---

### Coordination Games
#### Key idea
Firms benefit from choosing the same strategy.

#### Example
Boeing and Airbus choosing:
- Alpha technology
- Beta technology

#### Important insight
There may be:
- Multiple Nash equilibria
- Coordination problems

#### Benefits of communication
Pre-commitment communication can improve coordination.

#### Exam relevance
Useful for discussing:
- Standardization
- Technology adoption
- Platform competition

---

### Sequential Games and Backward Induction
#### Sequential Interaction
One firm moves first.

#### Backward Induction
The first mover predicts how the second mover will respond.

#### First-Mover Advantage
The first player may gain strategic advantages.

#### Example
Boeing chooses first:
- Airbus responds based on Boeing’s choice
- Boeing anticipates this response

#### Exam use
1. Start at the final stage
2. Identify rational response
3. Work backward to determine optimal initial decision

---

### Repeated Strategic Interaction
#### Key idea
Repeated interaction changes incentives.

Cooperation becomes more likely when:
- Long-term gains exceed short-term cheating gains
- The relationship is expected to continue

#### Important implication
Repeated interaction can support cooperation even without formal agreements.

---

### Mixed Strategy Equilibrium
#### Situation
No pure strategy equilibrium exists.

#### Example
- Kiana (principal)
- Len (agent)

Len chooses:
- Work
- Shirk

Kiana chooses:
- Monitor
- Not monitor

#### Result
- No stable pure strategy exists
- Players randomize strategies

#### Example from slides
Both randomize with probability 0.5.

#### Exam relevance
Important for:
- Principal-agent problems
- Monitoring systems
- Incentive conflicts

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Game theory:** Study of strategic interaction
- **Dominant strategy:** Best strategy regardless of rival actions
- **Nash equilibrium:** Stable outcome where no player wants to deviate
- **Coordination game:** Players benefit from matching choices
- **Sequential game:** Players move in sequence
- **Backward induction:** Solve game from the end backward
- **Repeated interaction:** Strategic interaction occurring multiple times
- **Mixed strategy:** Randomized strategy choice
- **First-mover advantage:** Advantage from acting first
