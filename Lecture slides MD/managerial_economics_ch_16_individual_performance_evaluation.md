# Lecture Notes: Chapter 16 – Individual Performance Evaluation

## 1. Overview
This lecture examines how firms evaluate employee performance and use performance measures to solve motivation and coordination problems. Key topics include objective and subjective performance measures, relative evaluation, opportunism, balanced scorecards, and team performance.

---

# 2. Main Notes

## Performance Evaluation

### Why firms evaluate performance
1. Provide employee feedback
2. Improve coordination
3. Determine rewards and sanctions
4. Support organizational architecture

### Important principle
Performance measurements must match:
- Compensation systems
- Decision rights

---

## Types of Performance Measurement

### Relative Performance Measures
Employees are evaluated relative to others.

### Absolute Performance Measures
Employees are evaluated independently.

### Objective Measures
Performance criteria are specified in advance and verifiable.

### Subjective Measures
Managers evaluate performance more holistically after the period ends.

---

## Incentive Compensation Model

### Employee output
Q = αe + μ

### Compensation
W = W₀ + βQ

### Key insight
Higher incentive coefficient β creates stronger incentives.

---

## Implicit Assumptions in Performance Measurement

The lecture emphasizes unrealistic assumptions behind simple models:
- Performance easy to measure
- One task/output only
- No gaming
- Output observed cheaply
- Employees work independently

Real organizations are much more complex.

---

## Setting Performance Benchmarks

### Methods
- Time and motion studies
- Past performance
- Standard benchmarks

### Ratchet System
Future targets are based on current performance.

### Ratchet Effect
Employees avoid exceeding targets because future expectations rise.

### Sandbagging
Employees intentionally underperform to create easier future targets.

---

## Measurement Costs

### Key idea
Better measurement systems are costly.

Firms should improve measurements until:

Marginal Benefit = Marginal Cost

---

## Informativeness Principle

### Definition
Use all low-cost information improving assessment of effort.

### Examples
- Market conditions
- Relative benchmarks
- External factors

### Benefits
- More precise evaluation
- Better incentives
- Lower employee risk premium

---

## Opportunism and Gaming

### Gaming
Employees manipulate measures instead of creating value.

### Horizon Problem
Employees focus on short-term results affecting immediate rewards.

### Example
Managers may sacrifice long-term investments to improve short-term bonuses.

---

## Balanced Scorecard

### Purpose
Avoid focusing only on financial performance.

### Four perspectives
1. Financial
2. Customer
3. Internal processes
4. Learning and growth

### Important insight
Using multiple measures reduces opportunism.

---

## Bonus Banks

### Purpose
Reduce short-term opportunism.

### Logic
Bonuses accumulate over time and can later be reduced if performance worsens.

This encourages long-term thinking.

---

## Jensen Critique of Budget-Based Bonuses

### Main argument
The problem is not budgeting itself.

The problem is linking bonuses directly to budgets/targets.

### Consequence
Employees game the system.

### Suggested solution
Linear pay-for-performance systems.

---

## Relative Performance Evaluation

### Definition
Employees are evaluated relative to peers.

### Advantages
- Filters out external shocks
- Better assessment of true effort

### Problems
- Employees may punish high performers
- Incentives to hire weaker coworkers
- Difficult comparisons across firms

---

## Subjective Performance Evaluation

### Why firms use it
Objective measures are imperfect.

Subjective evaluation captures:
- Cooperation
- Leadership
- Mentoring
- Difficult-to-measure behaviors

### Important quote
“You get what you measure.”

Meaning:
Employees focus on measured activities.

---

## Subjective Evaluation Methods

### Standard Rating Scales
Employees ranked on dimensions.

### Goal-Based Systems
Managers evaluate goal achievement.

### Peer Evaluation
Coworkers evaluate each other.

### Problems with peer evaluation
- Friendships
- Rivalries
- Bias

---

## Problems with Subjective Evaluation

### Supervisor Shirking
Managers avoid difficult evaluations.

### Forced Distribution
Employees forced into ranking distributions.

### Influence Costs
Employees spend resources influencing evaluators.

---

## Combining Performance Measures

### Key insight
Neither objective nor subjective measures are perfect.

Combining both usually creates better evaluations.

---

## Team Performance

### Why teams matter
Team production may create synergies.

### Advantages
- Cooperation
- Knowledge sharing
- Complementary skills

### Problems
- Free-riding

### Solution
Peer evaluations and repeated interaction.

# 4. Key Concepts

- **Objective Performance Measure:** Quantifiable and verifiable measure
- **Subjective Performance Measure:** Holistic managerial evaluation
- **Relative Performance Evaluation:** Evaluation relative to peers
- **Absolute Performance Evaluation:** Independent evaluation
- **Ratchet Effect:** Higher future targets after strong performance
- **Sandbagging:** Intentional underperformance
- **Gaming:** Manipulating performance measures
- **Horizon Problem:** Short-term focus
- **Balanced Scorecard:** Multi-dimensional performance system
- **Informativeness Principle:** Use all useful information
- **Forced Distribution:** Required ranking spread
- **Influence Costs:** Resources spent influencing evaluators
