# Lecture Notes: Chapter 22 – Ethics and Organizational Architecture

## 1. Overview
This lecture examines the relationship between ethics, incentives, and organizational architecture. It focuses on how firms can design systems that encourage ethical behavior while still maximizing firm value.

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Ethics and Organizational Architecture

Ethics are connected to:
- Reward systems
- Performance evaluation
- Decision rights

Organizational design can encourage:
- Ethical behavior
- Responsible decisions
- Long-term value creation

---

### Ethics and Business Choices

The course mainly uses a descriptive economic perspective:
- Individuals maximize their own well-being.

Business ethics instead focuses on:
- Normative questions
- What firms should do

---

### Examples of Ethical Issues

- Bribery
- Gifts to officials
- Misrepresentation of data
- Discrimination
- Boycotts
- Lack of diversity

---

### Value Maximization

#### Economic Darwinism
Efficient firms survive because they:
- Produce quality products
- Keep costs low

#### Main argument
Maximizing firm value may benefit stakeholders because it creates a larger economic surplus.

---

### Market Failure and Ethics

Markets may fail due to:
- Monopoly power
- Externalities

In these situations:
- Regulation may be necessary.
- Pure profit maximization may not be socially optimal.

---

### Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

#### Definition
Activities where firms consider broader social impacts.

#### Economic perspective
CSR should continue until:
- Marginal Benefit = Marginal Cost

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Ethics and Contracting Costs

#### Hirschleifer’s idea
Altruism reduces:
- Monitoring costs
- Enforcement costs

#### Meaning
Ethical behavior can reduce transaction costs inside firms.

---

### Organizational Architecture and Ethics

#### Main idea
Firms can design systems that encourage ethical conduct through:
- Incentives
- Monitoring
- Decision rights
- Corporate culture

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Ethics:** Standards of appropriate behavior.
- **CSR:** Corporate Social Responsibility.
- **Organizational Architecture:** Structure of incentives and decision rights.
- **Value Maximization:** Increasing firm value.
- **Economic Darwinism:** Efficient firms survive.
- **Externalities:** Costs or benefits imposed on others.
- **Transaction Costs:** Costs of monitoring and contracting.
- **Normative Analysis:** What firms should do.
- **Descriptive Analysis:** How individuals actually behave.

---
