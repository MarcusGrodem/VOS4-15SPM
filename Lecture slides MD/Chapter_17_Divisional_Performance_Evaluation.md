# Lecture Notes: Chapter 17 – Divisional Performance Evaluation

## 1. Overview
This lecture explains how firms evaluate the performance of different business units (divisions). It focuses on different organizational center types, transfer pricing, internal accounting, and the relationship between decision management and decision control.

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Measuring Divisional Performance
- Firms divide operations into different organizational units.
- Performance evaluations are linked to:
  - **Decision rights**
  - **Rewards**
  - **Responsibility**
- The accounting system measures group performance.

Important idea:
- Managers should only be evaluated on activities they can control.

---

### Cost Centers
- Focus on minimizing costs or maximizing output within a budget.
- Examples:
  - Manufacturing
  - Railroads
  - Hospitals

#### Key points
- No responsibility for revenue.
- Focus is operational efficiency.
- Quality may become a problem if managers focus only on cost reduction.

#### Important concept
Minimizing average cost does NOT necessarily maximize profits.

---

### Expense Centers
Examples:
- HR
- Accounting
- PR
- R&D

#### Characteristics
- Managers receive fixed budgets.
- Output is difficult to measure objectively.
- Risk of:
  - Overconsumption
  - Empire building
  - Larger-than-needed staff

---

### Revenue Centers
Examples:
- Sales divisions
- Distribution units

#### Goal
- Maximize revenue for a given budget.

#### Important limitation
Revenue maximization does not necessarily maximize profit.

Profit maximization happens when:
- MR = MC

Revenue maximization happens when:
- MR = 0

These are not the same unless MC = 0.

---

### Profit Centers
A combination of:
- Cost responsibility
- Revenue responsibility

Managers control:
- Product mix
- Selling prices
- Input mix

#### Benefits
Useful when managers possess specialized local knowledge.

#### Problems
- Divisions may maximize their own profit at the expense of the whole company.
- Possible:
  - Externalities
  - Free riding
  - Transfer pricing conflicts
  - Overhead allocation problems

---

### Investment Centers
Profit centers with authority over capital investments.

#### Evaluation methods
- Return on Assets (ROA)
- Economic Value Added (**EVA**)

#### Important limitation
Short-term accounting measures may discourage good long-term investments.

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Transfer Pricing (TP)

#### What it is
The price charged for goods or services transferred internally between divisions.

#### Why it matters
Transfer prices affect:
- Divisional profits
- Manager incentives
- Investment decisions
- Total company profits

#### Main principle
The optimal transfer price equals the **opportunity cost**.

---

### Transfer Pricing Methods

#### 1. Opportunity Cost Method
- Theoretically correct.
- Difficult to measure in practice.

#### 2. Market-Based Transfer Pricing
- Uses external market prices.
- Works best when competitive markets exist.

#### 3. Marginal Cost Pricing
- Transfer price = marginal cost.
- Efficient decisions possible.
- Problem:
  - Fixed costs may not be covered.

#### 4. Full Cost Pricing
- Easy to calculate.
- Common in practice.
- Risk:
  - Transfer prices become too high.
  - Output becomes too low.

#### 5. Negotiated Transfer Pricing
- Divisions negotiate internally.
- Often close to opportunity cost.
- Time-consuming.

---

### Internal Accounting

#### Decision Management
The process of:
1. Initiating decisions
2. Implementing decisions

#### Decision Control
The process of:
1. Ratifying decisions
2. Monitoring decisions

#### Important principle
If managers do not bear the wealth effects of decisions, decision management and decision control should be separated.

---

### Controllability Principle

#### Definition
Managers should only be held responsible for outcomes they can control.

#### Exam relevance
Used to evaluate whether performance systems are fair and efficient.

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Cost Center:** Unit focused on controlling costs.
- **Expense Center:** Unit with hard-to-measure outputs.
- **Revenue Center:** Unit focused on sales revenue.
- **Profit Center:** Unit responsible for both revenues and costs.
- **Investment Center:** Profit center with investment authority.
- **Transfer Pricing:** Internal pricing between divisions.
- **Opportunity Cost:** Value of the next best alternative.
- **Decision Management:** Initiation and implementation of decisions.
- **Decision Control:** Ratification and monitoring of decisions.
- **Controllability Principle:** Managers evaluated only on controllable outcomes.
- **EVA:** Economic Value Added.
