# Lecture Notes: Chapter 11 – Organizational Architecture

## 1. Overview
This lecture explains how firms design organizational systems to coordinate employees and align incentives. The chapter focuses on:
- Organizational architecture
- Decision rights
- Performance evaluation
- Reward systems
- Corporate culture
- Organizational change
- The controllability principle

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Organizational Architecture
Organizational architecture consists of three core elements:
1. **Decision rights**
2. **Performance evaluation**
3. **Reward systems**

These elements must work together.

#### Important metaphor
The slides compare architecture to:
- A three-legged stool

If one element changes:
- The others must also change.

---

### The Fundamental Problem
Important information inside firms:
- Is dispersed among many individuals
- Can be expensive to transfer

At the same time:
- Employees may have different incentives

This creates coordination and incentive problems.

#### Organizational architecture helps solve:
- Information problems
- Incentive conflicts
- Resource allocation issues

---

### Markets vs Firms
#### Markets
Markets coordinate through:
- Prices

#### Firms
Firms coordinate through:
- Assigned decision rights
- Control systems
- Incentives

Key challenge:
- Employees may not act in the firm’s best interest.

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Determinants of Organizational Architecture
Architecture depends on:

#### Strategy
- Price competition
- Quality
- Service

#### Technology
- Computers
- Telecommunications
- Production methods

#### Markets
- Competitors
- Customers
- Suppliers

#### Regulation
- Taxes
- Antitrust laws
- International regulation

#### Important insight
Strategy and architecture influence each other.

---

### Changing Organizational Architecture
Firms should adapt architecture when:
- The environment changes

However:
- Change is costly

#### Direct Costs
- Design costs
- Communication costs

#### Indirect Costs
- Employee resistance
- Changes in incentives
- System adjustment costs

#### Important principle
Benefits must exceed costs:
- MR > MC

---

### Interdependence of Architecture
The three architecture components are complements:
- Decision rights
- Rewards
- Performance evaluation

If one changes:
- The others should also change.

#### Example
Decentralization often requires:
- New reward systems
- New performance measures

---

### Corporate Culture
#### Definition
Culture consists of:
- Explicit and implicit behavioral expectations

#### Examples
- Rituals
- Role models
- Slogans
- Norms

#### Key idea
Architecture shapes culture.

#### Important insight
Culture can support incentive alignment.

---

### When Architecture Fails
Consequences may include:
- Manager dismissal
- Firm takeover
- Competitive decline

The lecture refers to:
- Economic Darwinism

Meaning:
- Inefficient organizations struggle to survive.

---

### Managerial Guide Questions
Managers should ask:

1. Does strategy fit the environment?
2. Does architecture fit strategy?
3. Are incentives aligned?
4. Does the system connect information with decision rights?
5. Does the control system fit decision rights?

---

### The Controllability Principle
#### Definition
Managers should only be evaluated on:
- Decisions they can control

#### Why important?
Avoids unfair evaluation and poor incentives.

#### Exam relevance
Very important principle in:
- Accounting
- Management control
- Organizational design

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Organizational architecture:** System of decision rights, rewards, and evaluation
- **Decision rights:** Authority to make decisions
- **Performance evaluation:** Measurement of employee performance
- **Reward systems:** Incentives and compensation systems
- **Corporate culture:** Shared norms and expectations
- **Controllability principle:** Employees should only be responsible for controllable outcomes
- **Economic Darwinism:** Inefficient firms fail over time
