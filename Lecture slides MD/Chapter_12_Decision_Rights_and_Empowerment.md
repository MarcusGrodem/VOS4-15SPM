# Lecture Notes: Chapter 12 – Decision Rights: The Level of Empowerment

## 1. Overview
This lecture focuses on how firms allocate decision-making authority inside organizations. Main topics include:
- Centralization vs decentralization
- Decision rights
- Empowerment
- Team decision making
- Decision management and control
- Influence costs

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Assigning Tasks and Decision Rights
Production processes involve:
- Tasks bundled into jobs

Two important job dimensions:

#### 1. Variety of Tasks
- Few tasks
- Many tasks

#### 2. Decision Authority
- Narrow authority
- Broad authority

#### Examples
- Specialists often have narrow authority
- Generalists often have broad authority

---

### Centralization vs Decentralization
#### Centralization
Decisions are retained at higher levels:
- CEO
- Top management

#### Decentralization
Decision rights are delegated:
- Local managers
- Teams
- Employees

#### Example: AutoMart
Question:
Who should determine prices?
- CEO?
- Local managers?
- Teams?

This determines where decision rights are placed.

---

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Benefits of Decentralization

#### Better Use of Local Knowledge
Local managers know:
- Customer preferences
- Local markets
- Price sensitivities

#### Conservation of Senior Management Time
Top managers can focus on:
- Strategy
- Long-term planning

#### Motivation and Training
Delegation can:
- Increase responsibility
- Improve motivation
- Develop future leaders

---

### Costs of Decentralization

#### Agency Problems
Local managers may:
- Pursue self-interest

#### Coordination Costs
Different units may:
- Fail to coordinate
- Duplicate activities
- Cannibalize sales

#### Loss of Scale Economies
Centralization may create:
- Lower costs through coordination

---

### Determining the Optimal Level of Decentralization
#### Model
Benefits:
- B × D

Costs:
- A × D
- C × D²

Where:
- D = degree of decentralization

#### Net Benefits
NB = BD − AD − CD²

Optimal decentralization:
D* = (B − A) / 2C

#### Interpretation
The optimal level depends on:
- Benefits of local knowledge
- Coordination costs
- Contracting costs

---

### Team Decision Making
#### Benefits
- Better use of dispersed knowledge
- Employee buy-in and commitment

#### Costs
- Slow decision making
- Free-rider problems
- Collective action problems

#### Important insight
Optimal team size balances:
- More knowledge
- Higher coordination costs

---

### Decision Management and Decision Control

#### Decision Management
- Initiating and implementing decisions

#### Decision Control
- Ratifying and monitoring decisions

---

### Four Decision Steps

#### 1. Initiation
Generating proposals

#### 2. Ratification
Approving proposals

#### 3. Implementation
Executing decisions

#### 4. Monitoring
Evaluating outcomes

---

### Separation of Decision Management and Control
#### Key principle
When decision makers do not bear the full wealth effects:
- Decision management and control should be separated.

#### Why?
To reduce:
- Opportunistic behavior
- Agency problems

#### Example
Boards of directors:
- Ratify CEO decisions
- Monitor management

---

### Influence Costs
Employees may attempt to:
- Influence managerial decisions

#### Costs include
- Time waste
- Lobbying
- Dysfunctional activities

#### Important insight
Bureaucratic rules may reduce influence costs.

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Decision rights:** Authority to make decisions
- **Centralization:** Decisions concentrated at top levels
- **Decentralization:** Delegation of authority downward
- **Empowerment:** Giving employees broader authority
- **Coordination costs:** Costs from managing decentralized units
- **Decision management:** Initiating and implementing decisions
- **Decision control:** Ratifying and monitoring decisions
- **Free-rider problem:** Individuals benefit without contributing fully
- **Influence costs:** Costs from lobbying and political behavior
