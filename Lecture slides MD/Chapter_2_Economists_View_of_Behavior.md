# Lecture Notes: Chapter 2 – Economists’ View of Behavior

## 1. Overview
This lecture explains how economists understand human behavior and decision-making. The chapter focuses on scarcity, opportunity costs, marginal thinking, incentives, utility maximization, indifference curves, budget constraints, and decision-making under uncertainty. It also introduces how economic reasoning can be applied to organizations and worker motivation.

---

## 2. Main Notes

### Economic Behavior
- People have **unlimited wants**, but resources are **limited**.
- Because resources are scarce, individuals must make **choices**.
- Economics studies how people allocate scarce resources among competing wants.

#### Key ideas
- Scarcity forces trade-offs.
- Every choice means giving up something else.
- Individuals try to choose the option that gives them the highest benefit.

---

### The Nature of Economic Choice
- Individuals choose preferred options subject to constraints:
  - Limited resources
  - Limited knowledge
  - Imperfect information
- Information is costly to obtain.
- Individuals learn from mistakes over time.

#### Important point
Economic decisions are rarely perfect because people never have complete information.

---

### Thinking at the Margin
- People think in terms of **marginal benefits** and **marginal costs**.
- **Marginal benefit (MB):** the additional benefit from a choice.
- **Marginal cost (MC):** the additional cost from a choice.

### Decision rule
Take action if:

- **MB > MC**

### Incremental thinking
- Economists focus on small additional changes.
- Important exam term: **incremental analysis**.

#### Example
Should you study one extra hour?
- MB = better exam preparation
- MC = less sleep or free time

If the benefit is larger than the cost, studying one more hour is rational.

---

### Sunk Costs
- **Sunk costs** are costs that already occurred and cannot be recovered.
- Sunk costs should not affect current decisions.

#### Key principle
“Bygones are forever bygones.”

#### Example
You drive three hours to a concert but forgot your ticket.
- The driving time is already spent.
- The rational decision depends only on future costs and benefits.

---

### Opportunity Costs
- All choices involve trade-offs.
- **Opportunity cost:** the value of the best alternative not chosen.

#### Examples
- Going to CBS instead of work
- Eating pizza instead of burgers
- Staying home instead of traveling

#### Important point
Opportunity costs are not always money.
They can include:
- Time
- Lost experiences
- Lost income
- Lost leisure

---

### Explicit and Implicit Costs
#### Explicit costs
Direct monetary payments.
- Rent
- Salary
- Tuition

#### Implicit costs
Opportunity costs without direct payment.
- Using your own time
- Giving up leisure
- Searching for information

---

### Utility Maximization
Individuals attempt to maximize utility.

- **Utility:** satisfaction or happiness gained from consumption.

Utility depends on combinations of goods such as:
- Food
- Clothing

---

### Indifference Curves
- Indifference curves show combinations of goods giving the same utility.

#### Characteristics
- Downward sloping
- Reflect trade-offs
- Higher curves represent higher utility

#### Important concept
The slope reflects willingness to substitute one good for another.

---

### Budget Constraint
A budget constraint shows what combinations of goods are affordable.

Formula:

I ≥ PfF + PcC

Where:
- I = income
- Pf = price of food
- Pc = price of clothing

#### Important point
- Points below the line are affordable.
- Points above the line are unaffordable.

---

### Changes in Income
- Higher income shifts the budget line outward.
- Lower income shifts it inward.

#### Interpretation
Income affects purchasing possibilities.

---

### Changes in Prices
- Price changes rotate the budget line.
- Higher clothing prices make the line steeper.
- Lower clothing prices make the line flatter.

---

### Combining Indifference Curves and Budget Constraints
Optimal choice occurs where:
- The budget constraint touches the highest possible indifference curve.

#### Important idea
Consumers maximize utility subject to limited resources.

---

### Price Changes and Consumer Choice
When food prices rise:
- Consumers buy less food.
- Consumption patterns change.

#### Two effects
1. **Substitution effect**
   - Consumers substitute toward relatively cheaper goods.

2. **Income effect**
   - Consumers effectively become poorer.

---

### Using Budgets to Motivate Workers
The lecture uses the Wells Fargo example.

#### Main idea
Compensation systems affect behavior.

- Analysts received bonuses connected to banking business.
- This created incentives to sacrifice integrity for money.

#### Economic insight
People respond to incentives.

Managers influence behavior by changing:
- Costs
- Benefits
- Rewards

---

### Alternative Models of Behavior
#### Happy-is-productive
- Employee satisfaction improves productivity.

#### Good citizen model
- Communication and praise matter.

#### Product of the environment
- Environment shapes behavior.

#### Economic model
Main model used in the course:
- Incentives matter.
- Individuals compare marginal benefits and costs.

---

### Decision Making Under Uncertainty
Future outcomes are uncertain.

#### Expected value
Decisions are based on expected outcomes.

Expected value depends on:
- Probability
- Outcome value

---

### Risk and Standard Deviation
- Risk is measured using **standard deviation**.
- Higher standard deviation means greater uncertainty.

#### Risk-return trade-off
People balance:
- Higher expected return
- Lower risk

---

### Risk Aversion
Risk-averse individuals prefer:
- Lower risk
- More certainty

#### Certainty equivalent
The guaranteed amount someone considers equal to a risky alternative.

#### Risk premium
Extra compensation required for accepting risk.

---

### Income and Substitution Effects in Labor Supply
When wages increase:
- Leisure becomes more expensive.
- But higher income can also increase demand for leisure.

#### Important insight
Higher wages do not always lead to more work hours.

## 3. Models and Frameworks

### Marginal Analysis Model
#### What it is
A decision-making framework comparing marginal benefits and marginal costs.

#### What it is used for
Determining whether an action should be taken.

#### How to apply in an exam answer
1. Identify additional benefits.
2. Identify additional costs.
3. Compare MB and MC.
4. Conclude whether the action is rational.

---

### Indifference Curves
#### What it is
A graphical model showing combinations of goods with equal utility.

#### What it is used for
Analyzing consumer preferences and trade-offs.

#### How to apply in an exam answer
- Explain utility maximization.
- Describe trade-offs.
- Identify optimal consumption point.

---

### Budget Constraint
#### What it is
A line showing affordable consumption combinations.

#### What it is used for
Analyzing effects of income and prices.

#### How to apply in an exam answer
- Explain affordability.
- Discuss price or income changes.
- Connect to consumer choices.

---

### Risk-Return Framework
#### What it is
A framework balancing expected value against uncertainty.

#### What it is used for
Understanding investment and risky decisions.

#### How to apply in an exam answer
- Define expected value.
- Explain risk using standard deviation.
- Discuss risk aversion.

---

## 4. Key Concepts

- **Scarcity:** limited resources relative to wants.
- **Marginal benefit:** additional benefit from a decision.
- **Marginal cost:** additional cost from a decision.
- **Opportunity cost:** value of best forgone alternative.
- **Sunk cost:** past cost that cannot be recovered.
- **Utility:** satisfaction from consumption.
- **Indifference curve:** combinations giving equal utility.
- **Budget constraint:** affordable combinations of goods.
- **Substitution effect:** consumers switch toward relatively cheaper goods.
- **Income effect:** purchasing power changes.
- **Risk aversion:** preference for lower risk.
- **Expected value:** probability-weighted outcome.
