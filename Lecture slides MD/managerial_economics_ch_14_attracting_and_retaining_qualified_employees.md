# Lecture Notes: Chapter 14 – Attracting and Retaining Qualified Employees

## 1. Overview
This lecture focuses on how firms attract, motivate, and retain employees through compensation systems and organizational design. Key themes include labor markets, human capital, compensating wage differentials, internal labor markets, promotions, efficiency wages, and salary versus fringe benefits.

---

# 2. Main Notes

## Contracting Objectives

- Firms design compensation contracts to maximize the value of employee output minus costs.
- Compensation systems should align employee interests with firm interests.
- Employees must receive at least their **reservation utility**.

### **Reservation Utility**
- The minimum level of satisfaction/pay an employee requires to accept a job.
- Equivalent to the value of the employee’s next-best alternative.

### Why compensation matters
Compensation is used to:
1. Attract employees
2. Retain employees
3. Motivate employees

---

## The Basic Competitive Model

### Assumptions of the model
- Labor markets are competitive
- Workers and jobs are identical
- Wages are determined by supply and demand
- Compensation is only monetary
- No long-term contracts
- Market wages are easy to observe

The lecture emphasizes that these assumptions are unrealistic but useful as a starting point.

### Firm hiring decision
A firm hires workers until:

**Marginal Revenue Product (MRP) = Market Wage**

### **Marginal Revenue Product (MRP)**
- The additional revenue generated by one more employee.

### Logic
- If MRP > wage → hire more workers
- If MRP < wage → reduce workers

---

## Relaxing the Assumptions

The real world differs from the basic model.

### Important differences
- Jobs are not identical
- Employees are not identical
- Information is costly
- Compensation includes non-monetary elements
- Jobs are often long-term relationships

This creates the need for more advanced compensation systems.

---

## Human Capital

### **Human Capital**
Knowledge, skills, training, and abilities that increase productivity.

### Two types

#### **General Human Capital**
- Useful across many firms
- Example: Excel skills, accounting knowledge
- Employees usually pay for this training themselves

#### **Specific Human Capital**
- Valuable mainly within the current firm
- Example: internal systems or company-specific routines
- Firms are more willing to pay for this training

### Exam relevance
The distinction explains:
- Why firms invest in some training but not others
- Why long-term employment relationships exist

---

## Compensating Wage Differentials

### Definition
Employees must receive higher pay for undesirable jobs.

### Examples of undesirable job characteristics
- Dangerous work
- Night shifts
- Stressful conditions
- Bad work environment

### Key idea
Different workers value job characteristics differently.
Some workers accept lower wages for better conditions.

---

## Internal Labor Markets

### Characteristics
- Entry-level hiring from outside
- Promotions mostly from within
- Long-term employment relationships

### Types of contracts

#### **Explicit Contracts**
- Formal written agreements

#### **Implicit Contracts**
- Informal expectations and understandings

### Why firms use internal labor markets
- Encourages employee loyalty
- Encourages firm-specific human capital investment
- Better matching of workers to jobs over time

---

## Long-Term Relationships (LTR)

### Benefits for firms
- More employee motivation
- More investment in firm-specific skills
- Reduced turnover
- Better employee-job matching

### Key point
Spot-market hiring is often costly in the long run.

---

## Pay in Internal Labor Markets

### Important idea
Firms may:
- Pay employees below MRP early in their career
- Pay employees above MRP later

This creates an incentive to stay with the firm.

### Upward-sloping earnings profile
Salary increases over time due to:
- Greater productivity
- Seniority
- Incentive effects

---

## Efficiency Wages

### Definition
Firms pay wages above market level when monitoring employee effort is difficult.

### Why firms use efficiency wages
- Reduce shirking
- Increase effort and loyalty
- Reduce corruption
- Increase quality

### Chicago Police example
The lecture highlights how high wages combined with penalties reduced corruption.

### Important exam idea
Efficiency wages increase the cost of losing the job, which motivates employees.

---

## Promotions as Tournaments

### Tournament theory
Employees compete against each other for promotions.

### Advantages
- Strong incentives
- Encourages high effort

### Problems
- Reduced cooperation
- Internal politics
- Influence costs
- Employees may value salary more than promotions

### **Peter Principle**
Employees may be promoted until they reach a level where they are incompetent.

---

## Compensation Components

Compensation consists of:
- Salary
- Fringe benefits

### Examples of fringe benefits
- Pension plans
- Insurance
- Healthcare
- Company car
- Extra vacation

### Important point
Salary and fringe benefits are not perfect substitutes.
Employees value them differently.

---

## Salary vs Fringe Benefits

### Why firms offer fringe benefits
- Tax advantages
- Quantity discounts
- Employees value benefits differently

### Optimal mix
The firm wants to:
- Meet employee reservation utility
- At the lowest possible cost

### Economic logic
Optimal compensation occurs where:
- Employee indifference curve is tangent to the firm’s isocost curve.

---

## Payroll Taxes and Compensation

Taxes can change the optimal balance between:
- Salary
- Fringe benefits

If wages are taxed more heavily than benefits, firms may offer more benefits.

---

## Attracting Different Employee Types

Different employees prefer different compensation packages.

### Example
- Single employees may prefer salary
- Married employees may value healthcare and insurance more

### Cafeteria-style benefits
Employees choose among benefit packages.

# 3. Models and Frameworks

## Competitive Labor Market Model

### What it is
A model explaining how wages and employment are determined.

### Used for
Understanding labor demand and hiring decisions.

### Exam application
1. Define MRP
2. Explain equilibrium where MRP = wage
3. Discuss assumptions and limitations

---

## Human Capital Framework

### What it is
Distinguishes between general and specific skills.

### Used for
Explaining training investments and long-term employment.

### Exam application
1. Define human capital
2. Compare general vs specific
3. Explain implications for firms and employees

---

## Efficiency Wage Theory

### What it is
Theory explaining why firms sometimes pay above-market wages.

### Used for
Reducing shirking and increasing productivity.

### Exam application
1. Define efficiency wages
2. Explain monitoring problem
3. Discuss benefits and drawbacks

---

## Tournament Theory

### What it is
Employees compete for promotions rather than direct pay increases.

### Used for
Creating incentives inside hierarchies.

### Exam application
1. Explain tournament structure
2. Discuss incentive effects
3. Evaluate drawbacks

---

# 4. Key Concepts

- **Reservation Utility:** Minimum acceptable utility/pay
- **Marginal Revenue Product (MRP):** Additional revenue from one extra worker
- **Human Capital:** Skills and knowledge increasing productivity
- **General Human Capital:** Transferable skills
- **Specific Human Capital:** Firm-specific skills
- **Compensating Wage Differential:** Extra pay for undesirable jobs
- **Internal Labor Market:** Promotion mainly from within
- **Efficiency Wage:** Wage above market level
- **Tournament Theory:** Employees compete for promotion
- **Fringe Benefits:** Non-salary compensation
- **Peter Principle:** Promotion to incompetence level
